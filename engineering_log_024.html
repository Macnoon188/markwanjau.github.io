Engineering Log #024

Version Compatibility

Evolving without breaking.

Date
5 July 2026
Objective
Design a version compatibility strategy that allows Jaspa to evolve its architecture, Provider SDK, and Knowledge Provider ecosystem without introducing breaking changes to existing providers or increasing the complexity of the Cognitive Core. The objective was not to prevent change — it was to make change predictable.

The Engineering Problem

Every successful software platform eventually encounters the same engineering dilemma. The architecture improves. New capabilities emerge. Interfaces become more expressive. Existing assumptions are refined. Yet every improvement introduces risk.

A change that benefits future providers may unintentionally invalidate those already deployed.

For Jaspa, this concern extended beyond source code. The platform's stability depended upon architectural contracts established throughout earlier milestones — Provider Manifests, Capability definitions, Canonical Data Models, KnowledgeObject structures, certification requirements, Acceptance Tests. Each represented an agreement between the Cognitive Core and every provider operating within its ecosystem.

Those agreements could not remain frozen forever. Equally, they could not change carelessly.

The platform needed to grow without asking every provider to grow at exactly the same pace.

Engineering Commentary

One of the earliest ideas considered was deceptively simple: whenever the architecture changed, require every provider to upgrade immediately. On paper, the approach appeared clean. In practice, it transferred architectural complexity directly to provider authors — the Cognitive Core would remain simple, but the ecosystem would become fragile.

The opposite approach appeared equally problematic — maintain indefinite compatibility with every historical interface. This gradually shifted complexity back into the platform itself: the Cognitive Core would accumulate special cases, translation logic would expand indefinitely, and architectural clarity would slowly erode.

Neither extreme aligned with Jaspa's engineering philosophy. Instead, compatibility became another architectural contract. The platform would evolve through intentional versioning rather than implicit behaviour. Providers would explicitly declare the architectural version they supported. The certification process would validate compatibility before deployment. Where adaptation became necessary, translation would occur at well-defined architectural boundaries rather than inside cognitive execution.

Evolution should occur at the edges of the architecture. Never at its centre.

Engineering Principles

Engineering Principle 025
Architectural contracts evolve deliberately.

Compatibility should be an intentional engineering decision supported by clearly defined contracts rather than an accidental consequence of implementation.

Engineering Principle 026
Versioning protects ecosystems, not software.

A version number has little value by itself. Its purpose is to preserve trust between independent engineers building against a shared architectural foundation.

Architecture Decision Record

ADR-019Accepted

Title

Introduce Explicit Architectural Version Compatibility

Context

The expanding Provider ecosystem required a structured strategy for supporting architectural evolution without disrupting certified providers or increasing Cognitive Core complexity.

Decision

Architectural contracts shall be versioned explicitly. Every provider shall declare its supported architectural version through its manifest. Compatibility validation shall occur during certification rather than during cognitive execution. Where translation is required, it shall occur at defined architectural boundaries.

Consequences

The platform gains predictable architectural evolution while preserving ecosystem stability. Future Provider SDK revisions become manageable without introducing special-case logic into the Cognitive Core.

Engineering Artifacts

Version Compatibility Architecture

Provider ↓ Provider Manifest (Architecture Version) ↓ Compatibility Validation ↓ ┌───────────┴───────────┐ ↓ ↓ Compatible Requires Update ↓ ↓ Capability Registry Certification Review ↓ Cognitive Planner

Version compatibility becomes an architectural responsibility rather than a runtime concern. The Planner should never need to determine whether a provider is compatible — that decision has already been made before the provider becomes available.

Architectural Evolution Strategy

Version 1.0 ↓ Provider Certification ↓ Stable Architectural Contracts ↓ Version 1.1 Introduced ↓ Compatibility Assessment ↓ ┌────┴────┐ ↓ ↓ Upgrade Continue └────┬────┘ ↓ Maintained Ecosystem

Rather than forcing synchronized upgrades, the architecture provides a controlled path through which providers and the platform evolve together. Stability becomes the default rather than the exception.

Lessons Learned

  • Compatibility is fundamentally an architectural concern, not an implementation detail.
  • Ecosystems evolve at different speeds — providers should not be forced into simultaneous upgrades.
  • Explicit contracts reduce uncertainty; a provider should never rely on undocumented behaviour.
  • Architectural stability is measured by the ability to accommodate change without disrupting existing systems.

// The Next Question

Compatibility ensures that providers can continue participating in the ecosystem as the architecture evolves. It does not address what happens when providers fail unexpectedly.

Networks become unavailable. External services time out. Knowledge sources degrade. Failures are inevitable.

The engineering challenge is not preventing every failure, but ensuring that isolated failures do not compromise the stability of the cognitive platform itself.

The next milestone would therefore focus on Failure Isolation — designing an architecture capable of degrading gracefully while preserving the integrity of the Cognitive Core.
