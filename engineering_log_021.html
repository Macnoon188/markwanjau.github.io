Engineering Log #021

Cognitive Telemetry

Measuring cognition.

Date
1 July 2026
Objective
Design a telemetry architecture capable of measuring the operational health of Jaspa's cognitive platform by capturing meaningful architectural metrics rather than conventional infrastructure statistics. The objective was not to collect more data — it was to collect the right data.

The Engineering Problem

Engineering Log #020 introduced architectural observability. Every significant decision made by the Cognitive Core could now be inspected through structured event streams. That milestone provided visibility.

Visibility alone, however, does not reveal trends.

An engineer investigating a single request may understand precisely why the Planner selected a particular provider. What remains invisible is whether that behaviour represents an isolated occurrence or an emerging pattern.

Suppose the Planner begins selecting the Memory Provider more frequently than anticipated. Is this expected behaviour? Has the quality of external knowledge deteriorated? Is contextual memory becoming too influential?

Suppose Knowledge Fusion begins rejecting a growing percentage of provider responses. Does this indicate conflicting evidence? Has trust scoring become overly restrictive?

Individual observations answer isolated questions. Engineering telemetry identifies long-term behaviour.

Engineering Commentary

Traditional telemetry platforms are exceptionally good at describing computers — processor utilisation, memory consumption, disk throughput, request latency, error rates. Those measurements explain how healthy the infrastructure is. They reveal remarkably little about whether the architecture is improving.

Consider two cognitive requests. Both complete successfully, consume identical computational resources, and return within two seconds. From an infrastructure perspective, they appear equivalent. Architecturally, they may be fundamentally different.

One request may require only a single provider. The other may orchestrate five providers, perform Knowledge Fusion, resolve conflicting evidence, lower confidence because of weak trust metadata, and ultimately produce a more reliable conclusion.

Infrastructure telemetry records identical outcomes. Architectural telemetry reveals entirely different cognitive journeys.

The Planner was no longer simply selecting capabilities — it was generating measurable planning behaviour. Knowledge Providers were no longer simply returning information — they were contributing measurable characteristics describing the health of the knowledge ecosystem.

Engineering Principles

Engineering Principle 019
Measure architecture, not merely infrastructure.

Healthy servers do not necessarily produce healthy cognitive behaviour. Meaningful telemetry measures the effectiveness of architectural decisions rather than the efficiency of hardware alone.

Engineering Principle 020
Every architectural metric should support an engineering decision.

Metrics collected without purpose eventually become operational noise. Every measurement should exist because it helps engineers understand, validate, or improve the architecture.

Architecture Decision Record

ADR-016Accepted

Title

Adopt Architecture-Centric Telemetry

Context

As Jaspa matured, conventional infrastructure metrics became insufficient for evaluating platform health. The engineering team required long-term measurements describing planner behaviour, provider utilisation, orchestration efficiency, trust evolution, and knowledge quality.

Decision

Introduce a telemetry layer responsible for aggregating architectural metrics independently of runtime execution. Telemetry shall consume structured architectural events and transform them into long-term engineering insights. Infrastructure monitoring shall remain separate from architectural telemetry.

Consequences

Operational dashboards become capable of representing cognitive health rather than merely infrastructure status. Future optimisation efforts can be guided by measurable architectural evidence rather than isolated observations.

Engineering Artifacts

Telemetry Architecture

Cognitive Events ↓ Telemetry Collector ↓ ┌───────────────────┼────────────────────┐ ↓ ↓ ↓ Planning Metrics Provider Metrics Trust Metrics └───────────────────┼────────────────────┘ ↓ Engineering Dashboard ↓ Long-Term Trend Analysis

Unlike observability, which explains individual execution paths, telemetry aggregates behaviour across thousands of executions. The objective is not to understand a single decision. It is to understand whether the architecture itself is evolving as expected.

Representative Cognitive Metrics

// Cognitive Telemetry
  • Planner Decisions / Hour
  • Capability Selection Distribution
  • Provider Utilisation
  • Average Execution Depth
  • Knowledge Fusion Frequency
  • Reflection Iterations
  • Average Trust Score
  • Evidence Acceptance Rate
  • Memory Recall Success
  • Provider Failure Rate
  • Acceptance Test Stability

Notice what is absent: CPU utilisation, memory consumption, network throughput. Those measurements continue to exist within traditional infrastructure monitoring.

Architectural telemetry deliberately focuses on the health of cognition rather than the health of the underlying hardware.

Lessons Learned

  • Architectural maturity requires measurable behaviour — otherwise optimisation quickly becomes subjective.
  • Telemetry should remain closely aligned with architectural principles, not arbitrary implementation detail.
  • Trends often reveal problems long before failures become visible.
  • Telemetry reinforces engineering discipline by letting engineers compare measurable behaviour before and after each design decision.

// The Next Question

Observability explains behaviour. Telemetry measures behaviour. Neither, however, enables the architecture to recognise when something has gone wrong.

If Jaspa can observe itself and measure itself, should it also be capable of diagnosing itself?

The next milestone would explore whether a cognitive operating system could perform structured self-diagnostics — identifying architectural inconsistencies, degraded providers, and operational anomalies before they became engineering problems.
