Engineering Log #016

The Trust and Evidence Framework

Building trust into Jaspa's reasoning.

Date
30 June 2026
Objective
Design a formal Trust Framework so that every response Jaspa produces carries measurable confidence and traceable evidence — shifting the platform from simple answer generation to evidence-aware reasoning.

Introduction

As Jaspa's reasoning capabilities continued to mature, a new architectural concern became impossible to ignore.

The system was becoming increasingly capable of retrieving information from multiple knowledge providers, synthesizing responses, and presenting coherent answers. While this represented a significant leap in capability, it also introduced an important engineering challenge:

How does Jaspa know when it should trust its own conclusions?

Most AI assistants present responses with the same level of confidence regardless of how they were produced. Whether an answer comes directly from authoritative documentation, a cached memory, or an inferred conclusion, the presentation often appears identical to the user.

For an engineering platform, that behavior is unacceptable. Jaspa must not only generate answers — it must communicate the confidence and evidence behind those answers.

The Problem

Consider three scenarios: a response generated directly from official vendor documentation, a response assembled from multiple knowledge providers, and a response inferred through reasoning because no direct evidence exists.

All three may sound equally convincing. However, they are not equally trustworthy.

Without a mechanism for expressing confidence, Jaspa risks presenting assumptions as facts.

The goal therefore became clear: every response should carry information about how trustworthy it is.

Engineering Objectives

The Trust Framework was designed around five principles:

  • Every response should include measurable confidence.
  • Every factual claim should be traceable to its origin.
  • Reasoning steps should remain transparent.
  • Multiple evidence sources should increase confidence.
  • Jaspa should openly acknowledge uncertainty rather than conceal it.
Engineering Principle 012
Confidence must be measurable, not implied.

This shifts the platform from simple answer generation to evidence-aware reasoning.

Trust Metadata

Every provider response is accompanied by metadata describing its origin.

provider: Bible Knowledge Provider source: Genesis 1:1 evidence_type: Direct Reference confidence: 1.00 timestamp: 2026-06-30T10:15:00Z

Rather than returning plain text alone, Jaspa now treats every response as a structured object containing both content and supporting context.

Confidence Levels

To make confidence understandable across all providers, standardized trust tiers were introduced.

ConfidenceInterpretation
1.00Verified from authoritative source
0.90Multiple providers agree
0.75Strong inference supported by evidence
0.50Partial evidence available
0.25Weak inference
0.00No supporting evidence

These values provide a common language for evaluating results regardless of which provider generated them.

Evidence Traceability

Every response should answer an additional question:

"Where did this information come from?"

Instead of producing isolated text, Jaspa records the supporting evidence used during reasoning.

Response ↓ ├── Provider ├── Source ├── Confidence ├── Timestamp └── Supporting References

This traceability simplifies debugging while allowing future providers to participate in collaborative reasoning without sacrificing transparency.

Handling Uncertainty

A trustworthy system should never fabricate certainty. If evidence is incomplete, Jaspa communicates that limitation explicitly. For example:

"Based on the available providers, this conclusion is supported with moderate confidence. Additional authoritative sources were not available."

Acknowledging uncertainty is a feature — not a weakness.

Preparing for Collaborative Reasoning

Although the immediate implementation focused on single-provider responses, the Trust Framework was intentionally designed with future expansion in mind.

As additional providers are introduced, Jaspa will be able to compare independent responses and adjust confidence dynamically.

Gemini \ \ Groq ----> Knowledge Fusion ----> Confidence Score / OpenRouter

Agreement between independent providers strengthens confidence. Conflicting evidence lowers confidence and encourages further investigation.

This architecture establishes the foundation for collaborative reasoning introduced in later milestones.

Architectural Impact

The Trust Framework transforms Jaspa from a traditional AI assistant into an engineering system capable of explaining its own reasoning process.

Instead of merely answering questions, Jaspa now distinguishes between:

  • Verified facts
  • Supported conclusions
  • Reasonable inferences
  • Uncertain assumptions

This distinction becomes increasingly valuable as the platform grows to include multiple providers, autonomous agents, and external knowledge sources.

Lessons Learned

  • Building intelligent systems is not simply about increasing model capability.
  • It is equally important to communicate the reliability of every conclusion.
  • Separating what Jaspa knows from how confidently Jaspa knows it improves transparency and debuggability.
  • Trust is not an optional feature layered on after deployment.
  • It is an architectural principle embedded into every reasoning decision Jaspa makes.
