Engineering Log #022

Architectural Self-Diagnostics

Teaching the platform to inspect itself.

Date
5 July 2026
Objective
Design a self-diagnostic framework capable of continuously evaluating the architectural health of Jaspa OS by identifying inconsistencies, degraded components, and structural anomalies before they become operational failures. The objective was to enable the architecture to reason about its own operational integrity.

The Engineering Problem

Every engineering system eventually experiences change. Providers evolve. Knowledge indexes grow. Capabilities are introduced. Configuration changes. Dependencies shift.

Each subsystem already possessed independent validation mechanisms. The Registry verified provider registration. Knowledge Providers validated manifests. Acceptance Tests confirmed architectural compliance. Telemetry exposed long-term behavioural trends.

Yet these mechanisms shared an important characteristic.

They were reactive. An engineer first needed to observe unusual behaviour before beginning an investigation.

Suppose a provider continued responding successfully while gradually producing lower-quality KnowledgeObjects. Suppose trust metadata became increasingly incomplete after several provider updates. Suppose the Capability Registry contained duplicate registrations that technically remained valid but introduced unnecessary planning ambiguity.

None of these situations necessarily represented immediate failures. Collectively, however, they indicated that the architecture was drifting away from its intended state.

Software usually reports failures. Mature platforms recognise deterioration long before failure occurs.

Engineering Commentary

Traditional diagnostic systems often focus on infrastructure — disk utilisation, network connectivity, memory exhaustion, database availability. These remain essential operational measurements. They reveal little about architectural correctness.

A server may operate flawlessly while the Planner repeatedly selects suboptimal capabilities. Every provider may remain online while trust metadata slowly degrades. Knowledge Fusion may continue producing responses while silently rejecting increasing amounts of evidence.

Infrastructure health and architectural health are related. They are not equivalent.

Every major subsystem already embodied engineering assumptions. The Planner assumed certified capabilities. The Registry assumed unique provider identities. Knowledge Providers assumed Canonical Data Model compliance. The Reflection Layer assumed complete trust metadata. The diagnostic framework would simply evaluate whether these assumptions continued to hold true during normal operation.

Importantly, diagnostics would never modify the architecture. Their responsibility would be limited to observation, evaluation, and reporting.

Diagnosis became an architectural responsibility. Repair remained an engineering responsibility.

Engineering Principles

Engineering Principle 021
Healthy architecture should continuously verify its own assumptions.

Architectural assumptions inevitably become invisible over time. Self-diagnostics periodically make those assumptions explicit, allowing engineers to detect structural drift before it becomes operational failure.

Engineering Principle 022
Diagnosis should precede intervention.

Automatically correcting behaviour without first understanding its cause risks introducing greater architectural instability. Good engineering begins with accurate diagnosis.

Architecture Decision Record

ADR-017Accepted

Title

Introduce Continuous Architectural Self-Diagnostics

Context

As Jaspa's architecture expanded, engineering confidence increasingly depended upon verifying that architectural assumptions remained valid throughout normal operation. Existing observability and telemetry systems provided valuable information but relied upon engineers interpreting operational data manually.

Decision

Introduce a dedicated Self-Diagnostic Framework responsible for continuously evaluating architectural integrity through predefined health checks and validation rules. The framework shall evaluate architectural conditions without participating in cognitive execution. Diagnostic results shall inform engineers rather than automatically modifying runtime behaviour.

Consequences

The architecture gains continuous structural awareness while preserving clear separation between diagnosis and execution. Future maintenance becomes increasingly proactive rather than reactive.

Lessons Learned

  • Architecture inevitably accumulates assumptions — they may remain correct for years, but they should never remain unverified.
  • Proactive diagnosis reduces operational complexity by detecting drift before users experience failures.
  • Engineering confidence depends upon independent verification — subsystems should not declare themselves healthy.
  • Mature platforms distinguish between understanding a problem and solving it.
